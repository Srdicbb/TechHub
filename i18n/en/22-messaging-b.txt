Delivery guarantees
@957eefceec
Networks and processes fail, so the system chooses: it risks a loss (**at-most-once**) or a duplicate (**at-least-once**). **Exactly-once** is built in practice as at-least-once plus idempotent processing.
@fa704277f6
At-most-once: the acknowledgement (ack) before processing. A crash after the ack means a lost message.
@b7dee3c268
At-least-once: the acknowledgement after processing. A crash before the ack means the same message arrives again.
@8b469db4b1
Idempotency: repeated processing does not change the result. Usually through a table of processed IDs (an inbox) in the same transaction as the effect.
@805b5b3541
Some operations are idempotent by themselves: "set the status to Paid", an upsert by key. These are not: "add 100 to the balance", "send an email".

Retry, backoff and DLQ
@5c005e1b2d
Transient errors are handled by retrying with ever longer gaps (**exponential backoff**) and a random offset (**jitter**). A message that still fails after N attempts goes to the **dead-letter queue** so it does not block the others.
@2816255176
The gap grows exponentially: 0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s, 4 s. An overloaded dependency gets time to recover.
@c0a33293e6
Jitter spreads the attempts. Without it, everyone who failed together hits again at the same moment.
@318a3467e0
A poison message (a bad format, a nonexistent entity) will never succeed. After `MaxDeliveryCount` attempts it goes to the DLQ.
@7c56e5e0cd
The DLQ is a queue for people and tools: an alert on the message count, insight into the reason, resending after the fix.
@58ae4454e4
Retry only transient errors (timeout, 503, 429). Send a validation error to the DLQ right away.
